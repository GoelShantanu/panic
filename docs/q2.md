## 1. The Question

> **Does StockPanic let users vote on what news *means for a stock's price* (bullish/bearish), or only on *whether the news is any good* (important / duplicate / wrong-stock / spam)?**

The reference product does the first. `[RE §4.4]` `[VERIFIED]` CryptoPanic ships **11 vote types** across four orthogonal axes — market direction (positive/negative/neutral), editorial salience (important/lol), moderation (toxic/flag/disagree), and personal utility (save/like/dislike/tip/thanks).

**OQ-2 asks only about the first axis.** Axes 2–4 are uncontroversial and in scope regardless.

---

## 2. Why This Decision Matters

### 2.1 It is the compliance decision, wearing a product costume

`[VERIFIED]` The regulatory perimeter (`phase-01-product-research.md` §6.1):

- SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations 2013, **last amended 25 Nov 2025** — no person may act as or hold out as an investment adviser without registration.
- **Circular 29 Jan 2025** — registered intermediaries are **barred from associating** with unregistered persons who give investment advice or make performance claims.
- Rules prohibit **specific buy/sell/hold calls, coded references to securities, and real-time trading cues** without registration.
- Enforcement is live: finfluencer Avadhut Sathe barred, **₹546 crore impounded**.

`[INFERRED]` Directional voting creates a platform where **thousands of unregistered users make public directional calls on regulated securities, which we then aggregate, rank, and distribute.** "Bullish" adjacent to a ticker is the textbook coded reference. That the label is crowd-sourced rather than authored is not obviously a defence — the question is what a user reasonably understands *"78% bullish on TATAMOTORS"* to mean, and every user understands it to mean "buy."

### 2.2 It may disqualify us from our best distribution channel

`[INFERRED]` The 29 Jan 2025 circular bars **registered intermediaries** from associating with unregistered advice-givers. If we host crowd directional calls, a broker may be unable to partner with us, embed us, or license our API.

**This is the sharpest point in the brief.** OQ-5 asks whether the B2B API is a core business. **OQ-2 may decide OQ-5 by making it impossible.** Compliance here is not a tax on the business — for the B2B line it *is* the business.

### 2.3 It is a manipulation surface with money on the other side

`[INFERRED]` A coordinated group votes a small-cap "bullish"; our UI ranks it trending; we have built a pump amplifier wearing our credibility badge. `[RE §29.3]` The reference product's answer is **attributable voting** — public voter identities raise the cost of brigading. That mitigation is available to us, but it does not address §2.1 or §2.2 at all.

### 2.4 What depends on it

| Downstream | Dependency |
| --- | --- |
| **Schema (OQ-4)** | Directional votes must aggregate *somewhere*. OQ-4 proposes making tone→ticker **structurally unrepresentable**. A bullish vote **is** a tone→ticker edge. **OQ-2 and OQ-4 are the same decision viewed from two angles — they cannot be answered inconsistently.** |
| **Ranking** | `[RE §4.4]` Crowd labels drive *rising*, *hot*, *important*. Whatever we collect becomes the ranking substrate. |
| **B2B (OQ-5)** | §2.2 — may be foreclosed. |
| **Entity resolution** | A "wrong stock tagged" vote is a **direct training signal** for the resolver — an enabling capability under D-009. A "bullish" vote trains nothing we own. |
| **Moderation economics** | `[RE §29.2]` At consumer price points there is no editorial desk. Crowd labour is the substitute. Whichever labels we collect are the ones we get for free. |

## 3. Options

### Option A — Full directional voting *(CryptoPanic parity)*

Bullish / bearish / neutral on securities, aggregated and rankable.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Proven engagement mechanic. Generates a sentiment layer we could sell. Familiar to anyone who has used the reference product. |
| **Disadvantages** | Squarely inside the §2.1 perimeter. Pump amplification surface. |
| **Technical** | Simple — a vote table with a direction enum. |
| **Product** | Adds a signal users like. Directly contradicts D-009's *"information quality over engagement."* |
| **Business** | 🔴 May foreclose broker/B2B distribution entirely (§2.2). |
| **Cost** | Low to build; **potentially unbounded legal cost**. |
| **Complexity** | Low technically; high regulatory. |
| **Risk** | 🔴 **Severe.** Precedent is a ₹546 cr impound. Requires GUARDRAILS §4.3/§4.4 to be rewritten. |

### Option B — Quality voting only *(Phase 1 recommendation)*

Important / not-important · duplicate · **wrong stock tagged** · spam/paid-promotion · old news.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | No directional claim on any security → outside the §2.1 perimeter. **"Wrong stock tagged" trains the entity resolver.** Preserves the crowd-labour subsidy. Keeps B2B open. |
| **Disadvantages** | Less emotionally engaging. No sentiment layer to sell. Users may expect bullish/bearish from competitor familiarity. |
| **Technical** | Same complexity as A. |
| **Product** | Directly serves D-009: *"high-signal"* is a quality judgement, and this is exactly the label for it. |
| **Business** | Keeps every distribution channel open. |
| **Cost** | Low. |
| **Complexity** | Low. |
| **Risk** | 🟢 Low. Main risk: weaker engagement than A. |

### Option C — Directional voting, private and non-aggregated

Users mark bullish/bearish for **their own** filtering. Never displayed to others, never aggregated, never ranked.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Personal utility with no public directional claim. Arguably a private note, not advice. |
| **Disadvantages** | `[INFERRED]` The compliance position is **untested and thinner than it looks** — we would still be collecting and storing directional opinions on securities per user. And a private label generates no crowd signal, so we pay the schema cost for none of the benefit. |
| **Technical** | Same table, plus a hard visibility constraint. |
| **Product** | Marginal value. Users can already save/filter. |
| **Business** | Neutral. |
| **Cost** | Low. |
| **Complexity** | Medium — the "never aggregate" rule must be enforced structurally, forever, against future engineers. |
| **Risk** | 🟠 Medium. **Worst of both:** carries the schema hazard OQ-4 exists to prevent, buys almost nothing. |

### Option D — Quality voting now, directional deferred pending counsel

Ship B. Keep A as a live question for OQ-8 counsel. Decide later with legal input.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Ships the safe, useful thing immediately. Doesn't foreclose A permanently. Honest about the CTO not being a lawyer. |
| **Disadvantages** | `[INFERRED]` Deferral is **partly illusory**. If we later add directional voting we would need to revisit the schema (OQ-4), ranking, and any B2B contracts signed in the interim. The cost of adding it later is higher than the cost of adding it now. |
| **Technical** | Identical to B today. |
| **Product** | Identical to B today. |
| **Business** | Keeps B2B open today; preserves optionality on a sentiment product. |
| **Cost** | Low now; migration cost later if we reverse. |
| **Complexity** | Low. |
| **Risk** | 🟢 Low. |

### Option E — Quality voting + *non-directional* magnitude signal

Option B, plus a crowd signal about **how much something matters** — not which way it points. `[RE §4.3.2]` The reference product's source-count badge (*"14 sources reported this"*) is a verified instance: a magnitude signal derived from aggregation, not opinion.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Recovers much of A's *utility* (what's big right now) with none of its exposure. "Important" and "14 sources reported this" are **volume facts**, not directional characterisations — permitted under Research §6.3. Makes dedup perceivable, which D-009's consequence list flags as necessary or we look like Pulse. |
| **Disadvantages** | Still no directional sentiment. More design work to make magnitude feel as satisfying as direction. |
| **Technical** | B + cluster-size surfacing. Cluster size is a **byproduct of dedup we get for free**. |
| **Product** | Strongest fit with D-009. "Never miss what matters" needs a *matters* signal; magnitude is that signal, and it is compliant. |
| **Business** | Keeps all channels open. |
| **Cost** | Low — the data already exists as a dedup byproduct. |
| **Complexity** | Low–medium. |
| **Risk** | 🟢 Low. |

## 4. The Compliance Reality — Stated Plainly

`[ASSUMPTION]` **The CTO is not a lawyer and this brief is not legal advice.** The §2.1 reading is an `[INFERRED]` interpretation of published regulation by an engineer. It may be wrong in either direction — SEBI may treat crowd sentiment more permissively than assumed, or less.

`[VERIFIED]` What is not in doubt: the regulations exist, they were amended within the last 8 months, the 29 Jan 2025 circular constrains intermediary association, and enforcement has reached ₹546 crore against a single actor.

**GUARDRAILS §3.5 already gates AI-layer implementation on retaining SEBI-competent counsel (OQ-8).** Directional voting belongs in the same conversation. The cost of counsel is trivial against rebuilding the vote system, the schema, and any B2B contracts.

## 5. Recommendation

### **Option E — Quality voting + non-directional magnitude signal.**

### 6. Why

**It is the only option that serves D-009's promise directly.** *"Never miss high-signal news that matters to you"* requires a *matters* signal. Direction is not that signal — **magnitude** is. "14 sources reported this within 20 minutes" answers "does this matter?" better than "63% of strangers think it goes up," and it is a volume fact rather than a directional claim.

**The compliant design is also the commercially superior one — genuinely, not as consolation.** Directional votes train nothing we own. **"Wrong stock tagged" trains the entity resolver**, which D-009 names an enabling capability of the core promise. We would be swapping a signal that creates legal exposure for one that compounds into our product. `[RE §29.2]` establishes that crowd labour is what makes ranking and moderation affordable at consumer price points; Option E simply points that labour at something useful.

**It keeps the best business open.** §2.2 — if the B2B API is core (OQ-5), Option A may make us untouchable to the exact partners we need. Option E costs us nothing there.

**Magnitude is free.** Cluster size falls out of deduplication as a byproduct. We are already building it. `[RE §4.3.2]` demonstrates the pattern in production, and D-009's consequences already flag that dedup must be *perceivable* or our differentiation is invisible against Pulse. **Option E solves the compliance question and the perceptibility question with one badge.**

### The honest cost of E

**We lose a genuinely engaging mechanic.** Directional voting is fun, it is social, and users arriving from CryptoPanic-like products will expect it. We are betting that *"14 sources are reporting this"* is a satisfying enough substitute for *"the crowd is bullish."* **That bet is unproven.** If it fails, the feed feels informative but inert.

**We also forgo a sellable sentiment dataset** — a real asset, and a plausible B2B product in its own right, which Option A would generate and E will not.

### What would change this recommendation

Counsel advising that aggregated, attributed, clearly-labelled crowd sentiment sits **outside** the IA Regulations perimeter. If so, Option A's engagement and data value become available and OQ-2 should be reopened. **This is exactly the question to put to OQ-8 counsel.**

## F.1 The Decision

**Community Opinion** — a lightweight community interaction layer allowing authenticated users to express **their own interpretation of a news story**.

- One opinion per user per **canonical story**: 🟢 Bullish · 🔴 Bearish · ⚪ Neutral
- Raw vote counts only. Never percentages. Never consensus. Never "Market Sentiment."
- Labelled only as **"Community Opinion."**
- **Never influences** ranking, feed ordering, importance, alerts, notifications, AI summaries, recommendations, trending, search ranking, or personalisation.
- Purely user-generated metadata attached to a canonical story.

**Quality Voting remains separate**, with a different purpose (data quality) and different treatment (it *does* drive ranking).

---

## F.2 CTO Position — Recorded for the Record

**I do not consider this a mistake.** Two things moved the assessment:

1. **The framing is load-bearing and it is the founder's, not the CTO's.** *"Users express their own interpretation of a news story"* — *"I read this story as bullish"* is not *"I am bullish on Tata Motors."* One is a reading of an article; the other is a position on a security. That distinction is legally and architecturally real.
2. **Intermediary safe harbour.** `[INFERRED]` Under IT Act §79 a platform hosting user-generated content may escape liability *if it acts as a passive conduit*. **The founder's ranking-isolation constraint is plausibly what preserves that passivity** — a platform that ranks by crowd bullishness curates directional opinion; one that displays an inert count is closer to a bulletin board. This is a stronger argument for F than any offered in its original proposal.

### ⚠ CTO retraction — the OQ-4 conflict was overstated

Part I and the preceding discussion asserted that Option F and OQ-4's strong form were *"mutually exclusive — a schema fact."* **That was wrong.**

If articles carry AI tone and articles carry ticker tags, then tone→ticker is already one JOIN away **without Community Opinion existing at all**. OQ-4's "structurally unrepresentable" was never achievable by schema design in any database where both facts coexist. The CTO held the founder's feature to a standard the CTO's own proposal could not meet.

The real control was never *"don't store it"* — it is **physical separation** (§F.3). That control serves OQ-4 equally. **Build it once.**

---

## F.3 Architecture — Isolation by Ignorance

**Design principle: make ticker-level aggregation impossible by ignorance, not by policy.** Policy erodes under growth pressure; a service that lacks the data cannot be pressured.

```
┌─────────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────────┐
│      MAIN PLATFORM          │        │      OPINION SERVICE         │
│                             │   ✗    │      (separate datastore)    │
│  canonical_story            │  no    │                              │
│  story_ticker  ◄── entity   │  join  │  opinion(                    │
│  article                    │ ─────► │    user_id,                  │
│  tone (AI)                  │  path  │    canonical_story_id, ◄─ opaque
│  ranking_inputs             │        │    direction,                │
│  quality_votes ──► ranking  │        │    created_at)               │
└─────────────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────────────┘
                                        Holds NO ticker, entity, or
                                        entity-graph data. Cannot
                                        compute a per-security view.
```

| # | Guardrail | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F.3.1** | **Separate service, separate datastore.** `canonical_story_id` is an opaque key. | The opinion service *cannot* compute "bullish count for TATAMOTORS" — it lacks the data. Aggregation would require a deliberate, reviewable, cross-service application join. Not possible in a query, by accident, or by a curious engineer. |
| **F.3.2** | **API surface: two endpoints, deliberately impoverished.** `GET /opinions/{story_id}` → `{bullish, bearish, neutral}`; `POST /opinions/{story_id}`. **No** list, per-ticker, aggregate, or time-series endpoint. | An endpoint that does not exist cannot be misused or leaked. Each absence is a decision. |
| **F.3.3** | **Excluded from the B2B/partner API entirely.** | Severs the §2.2 foreclosure risk at the root: a broker consuming our feed never receives crowd directional data, so the intermediary-association concern does not arise for them. **This alone protects OQ-5.** |
| **F.3.4** | **Ranking isolation enforced by credentials, not discipline.** No network route, no credentials from ranking → opinion store. CI test fails if any ranking-path module imports the opinion client. | *"Votes never influence ranking"* is a policy; a missing credential is a property. |
| **F.3.5** | **Server-side kill switch.** Global disable without deploy. | `[RE §19.2]` — the reference product's `if (t.data.url) → redirect` hook lets the server gate any action. Same instinct: if counsel says stop, we stop in one config change. |
| **F.3.6** | **Attributable voting** — public voter identity. | `[RE §29.3]` Raises brigading cost, and strengthens the reported-speech framing: these are *named individuals'* readings, not our anonymous aggregate. Load-bearing for safe harbour. |
| **F.3.7** | **Eligibility gate** — account age (~30d) or verified email + minimum activity. | `[RE Part IV, VERIFIED]` The reference product gates its `toxic` vote on PLUS membership **or** reputation ≥ 300 — money *or* earned standing, and money alone is insufficient (PLUS still needs rep ≥ 5). Proven pattern for a contested vote. |
| **F.3.8** | **Immutable audit log** — user, story, direction, timestamp, IP. | `[Research E7]` Required for abuse investigation and for answering a regulator. |
| **F.3.9** | **No time-series exposure.** Store `created_at` for abuse detection; never expose vote-over-time. | A time-series enables reconstructing exactly the sentiment trend this design excludes. |
| **F.3.10** | **One vote per user per canonical story** (not per duplicate article). | Founder constraint. Consistent with dedup; caps brigading damage. |

## F.4 UX / Display

### F.4.1 Copy discipline — enforce the founder's framing

The vote is on **the story's reading**, never on the security's prospects. The moment copy implies a position on a stock, the frame that makes this defensible collapses.

| ✅ Ship | ❌ Never |
| --- | --- |
| *"How do you read this story?"* | *"What's your view on TATAMOTORS?"* |
| **"Community Opinion"** | "Market Sentiment" / "Consensus" / "The crowd says" |
| Bound to the story card | Rendered adjacent to a live price |
| — | Any cross-story or cross-ticker ordering by opinion |

### F.4.2 Progressive Vote Display *(founder-approved, 2026-07-15)*

A display threshold before directional breakdown is shown.

| Total votes | Display | Breakdown shown? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0** | **"Be the first to vote."** | ❌ No — never render `Bullish (0) · Bearish (0) · Neutral (0)` |
| **1–2** | **"2 people voted"** *(or the appropriate total)* | ❌ No |
| **3+** | **Bullish (X) · Bearish (Y) · Neutral (Z)** | ✅ Yes |

**Rationale (founder):** avoids long rows of zero-value counters on a new platform while still encouraging participation; prevents tiny sample sizes from appearing more statistically meaningful than they are.

**CTO assessment:** endorsed. It directly mitigates the **cold-start problem** flagged in §F.5 — at launch, `"Be the first to vote"` reads as an *invitation* rather than an *abandonment signal*, which is the single largest execution risk to this feature. Zero architectural cost: a pure display function over data already held. No API change (`GET /opinions/{story_id}` returns the same payload; the client applies the threshold).

**Two things this does not do — recorded so they are not assumed closed:**

1. **It does not resolve the small-sample concern it cites.** At n=3, `Bullish (2) · Bearish (1)` still reads as "67% bullish" and is still a meaningless sample. The threshold suppresses only the trivially small cases (n≤2). A defensible statistical threshold would be closer to n≈10 — though no threshold is clean, and 3 is a reasonable product compromise. **The stated rationale overclaims; the UX benefit is real regardless.**
2. **It does not touch the high-end problem** (§F.6). This governs the *bottom* of the range. The open contradiction is at the *top*.

### F.4.3 Placement

Story detail card only. Not in the feed row. `[RE §29.1]` — the reference product keeps its full vote apparatus off the scanned surface and one route away, which is why a product with 11 vote types still reads as simple. Same reasoning applies: the feed serves D-009's promise; the crowd layer must not tax it.

## F.5 Timing — Immediately After MVP; Build in MVP, Ship Dark

**Recommendation: the architecture (§F.3) lands in MVP behind the §F.3.5 flag. The feature enables post-MVP on a DAU threshold.**

Reasoning is execution, not theory:

- **Cold-start math.** D-009 gives a live reverse-chron firehose — ~200+ canonical stories/day. At launch with a few hundred users, votes-per-story rounds to zero. §F.4.2 substantially mitigates the *symptom* (`"Be the first to vote"` beats `"Bullish (0)"`), but not the *cause*: a story that never reaches 3 votes never shows the feature at all.
- **Structural tension with D-009 — worth seeing plainly.** A firehose *disperses* attention; voting *needs concentration*. Reddit and HN work because attention pools on few items. `[INFERRED]` The reference product sustains a firehose + voting only on years of accumulated users. Rough gate: ~10+ votes on top stories across ~200 stories/day implies **thousands of votes/day**, i.e. tens of thousands of sessions. That is a mature product.
- **Don't let the only counsel-gated feature block launch.** It is the least core feature and the only one needing a lawyer (§F.7). Coupling them makes legal delay into launch delay.
- **MVP must prove the promise** — *never miss high-signal news, minimal effort* (D-009). Community Opinion serves none of it.
- **Dark-ship is nearly free** because §F.3.5 already exists. Enable via config when DAU clears the threshold. No migration.

## F.6 ⚠ OPEN — One Founder Decision Outstanding

**The spec contains a contradiction at scale.** *"Show only raw vote counts"* and *"never derive or display consensus"* cannot both hold.

At n=14, `Bullish (4) · Bearish (3) · Neutral (7)` reads as noise — correct.
At n=8,000, `Bullish (6,200) · Bearish (1,100)` **is** a consensus display. The reader divides. **A ratio cannot be withheld by declining to compute it.**

Raw counts are *worse* than percentages here: "85%" hides n; "6,200 vs 1,100" shows ratio **and** sample size — precisely what makes a figure look statistically weighty.

| Option | Consensus-proof at scale? | Feature value retained? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw uncapped counts *(current spec)* | ❌ No | ✅ Full |
| **Capped display** — e.g. `20+` | ✅ Yes | 🟡 Diminished but real |
| Buckets — "a few / some / many" | ✅ Yes | 🟡 Vague |
| Percentages | ❌ No | ✅ Full |

**CTO recommendation:** cap. It is the only variant that honours the stated intent. **Founder's call** — the ask is that it be chosen consciously rather than discovered at 8,000 votes.

§F.4.2 does **not** close this. It governs n≤2; this governs n≫100.

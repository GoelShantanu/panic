## 1. The Question

> **When, and in what form, do we sell our data to other businesses: brokers, fintechs, and registered research analysts/advisers?**

D-009 already settled that the **consumer stream is the primary product**. It rejected "B2B API-first" and "dual-track" as the *primary user* decision. OQ-5 does not reopen that. It asks a narrower question:

1. **Priority:** is the API a core roadmap item with its own timeline, or a premium add-on after launch?
2. **Content:** what may a partner feed contain, given D-011?
3. **Gate:** what must be true before we sign a paying B2B customer?

---

## 2. What Changed Since Research Recommended "Yes"

Research §3.3 and §11 called B2B "plausibly the best business". Three things have moved since.

### 2.1 The professional market is ~4× smaller than assumed (E-3)

`[VERIFIED, docs/q1.md §2]` SEBI-registered RAs + IAs number **~2,500**, not 10,000+. At ₹5k/month and 100% market share the ceiling is **≈₹15 crore/year**. At a realistic 10% share that is **≈₹1.5 crore/year**.

Broker/fintech API remains **"dozens of firms" at ₹1L+/month** (Research §3.3, `[INFERRED]`). Illustratively, 20 firms × ₹1–3L/month ≈ **₹2.4–7.2 crore/year**. That is real money, but it is a sales-led business with long cycles, not an automatic margin engine. `[ASSUMPTION]`: no firm has been asked whether it would pay.

### 2.2 D-011 put the best channel at legal risk

`[VERIFIED, Research §6.1]` The SEBI circular of 29 Jan 2025 bars registered intermediaries from **associating with** unregistered persons who give investment advice.

`[INFERRED]` Since D-011 the platform hosts aggregated crowd bullish/bearish calls on listed securities, and those calls may drive ranking. A broker's compliance team may read a partnership with us as association, **regardless of what the partner feed contains**. The circular's concern is who you associate with, not just which fields you receive. Feed hygiene (§4) reduces the exposure. It may not remove it. **Only counsel can say.**

This is the collision `docs/q2.md` §2.2 predicted: D-011 and OQ-5 pull against each other, and counsel's answer decides how hard.

### 2.3 Redistribution rights were never examined

`[ASSUMPTION]` — not researched, flagged for the first time here:

- **Exchange data.** Redistributing NSE/BSE announcements to paying third parties may need a **vendor/redistribution licence**, costing more than the ~₹3L/yr display feed in OQ-6.
- **Publisher content.** Showing a headline and linking out to a consumer is one thing. Selling headlines, snippets or summaries of third-party journalism to businesses is **a different rights question**. `[INFERRED]` CryptoPanic's API serves titles and URLs; whether that is licensed or tolerated is unknown.

Either could make a B2B feed **unsellable as specified**, independent of SEBI.

## 4. One Rule That Applies to Every Option Except D

Whatever the timing, a partner feed should carry **facts, not opinions**:

| ✅ Partner feed may contain | ❌ Partner feed must not contain |
| --- | --- |
| Deduplicated stories, source attribution, URLs, timestamps | User directional votes (D-011), in any form |
| ISIN-resolved instrument tags with confidence | Any ordering or ranking influenced by directional votes |
| AI event-type classification (D-014) | AI tone (none exists, D-014) |
| Tone-neutral attributed summaries, *subject to §2.3 rights* | Anything not cleared under §2.3 |
| Activity/volume facts (a Panic-Score-style measure; GUARDRAILS §4.4 permits volume facts) | |

This restores, for the B2B surface only, the partner-API exclusion that D-011 removed from Option F (`docs/q2.md` §F.3.3). It is the cheapest available mitigation for §2.2.

## 5. Options

### Option A — Core roadmap, commercial at MVP launch

API built in parallel with the consumer product; sales to brokers and fintechs start at launch.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Earliest revenue; B2B cash subsidises consumer growth (Research §11). |
| **Disadvantages** | Sells into the §2.2 association risk before counsel has answered. Rights (§2.3) unresolved. Runs the second motion D-009 rejected. |
| **Technical** | API-first architecture, partner auth, metering, SLAs, support. |
| **Cost** | High: sales effort, licences, contracts, on-call commitments. |
| **Risk** | 🔴 High. Contracts signed now may need unwinding if counsel says no. |

### Option B — Status quo: premium API ~90 days after launch

The original brief's sequence: API is a paid add-on once the consumer product runs.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Simple. Keeps MVP focus. |
| **Disadvantages** | Treats a structurally different business (licences, counsel, enterprise sales) as a feature. The architecture may not be API-ready, so this becomes a retrofit. Gate conditions are undefined. |
| **Cost** | Low now; retrofit later. |
| **Risk** | 🟠 Medium. The real risk is drifting into Option A without the gates. |

### Option C — API-first architecture now; commercial B2B gated on counsel, rights and traction

Build the platform so the web app consumes the **same API** a partner would. Define the partner feed per §4 from day one. **Do not sell** until three gates clear: counsel on §2.2, redistribution rights on §2.3, and consumer traction proving the pipeline (entity resolution and dedup quality).

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Near-zero extra cost: API-first is good architecture anyway. Keeps B2B fully open without signing into an unknown legal position. Respects D-009's one-motion focus. Each gate is concrete and checkable. |
| **Disadvantages** | No B2B revenue at launch. stockinsights.ai keeps the lane meanwhile. |
| **Technical** | API-first design; data model separates partner-visible from consumer-only fields; partner surface built but not opened. |
| **Cost** | Low. |
| **Risk** | 🟢 Low. Main risk: the gates take long, and B2B arrives late. |

### Option D — Consumer only; drop B2B

No partner API on the roadmap.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | Maximum focus. Makes D-011's legal exposure purely a consumer question. |
| **Disadvantages** | Discards the line Research rated highest-margin. Retail is anchored at ₹0 by Pulse (Research §7.2). Forecloses an option that costs little to keep. |
| **Risk** | 🟠 Medium: strategic, not legal. |

### Option E — B2B through a separate entity or brand

Sell the clean feed (§4) from a separate company or brand that hosts no crowd voting, so brokers associate with it rather than with the voting platform.

| Dimension | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Advantages** | `[INFERRED]` The most direct structural answer to §2.2, if counsel agrees separation is effective. |
| **Disadvantages** | Two entities, two brands, for one founder. Effectiveness is entirely a legal question. Premature before counsel has spoken. |
| **Cost** | High: incorporation, accounting, contracts. |
| **Risk** | 🟠 Medium. Worth holding as the fallback if counsel finds an association problem. |

## 6. Recommendation

### **Option C — API-first now; sell only after counsel, rights and traction gates clear. Hold E as the fallback.**

### Why

**It keeps the option without betting on it.** API-first architecture costs almost nothing extra and is what a well-built stream needs anyway. Everything expensive (sales, licences, contracts) waits for evidence.

**Three unknowns could each kill B2B as specified, and none is answered yet.** Counsel on association (§2.2), redistribution rights (§2.3), and whether our pipeline is actually better than stockinsights.ai's. Selling before those are answered is the expensive way to find out.

**It respects D-009.** Dual-track was rejected for founder bandwidth. C adds no second motion until the gates clear.

**It makes the D-011 trade-off explicit, not accidental.** If counsel says hosting crowd votes blocks broker partnerships, you choose with full information: Option E (separate entity), dropping B2B, or revisiting D-011. Under A that choice would arrive mid-contract.

**The §4 rule ships either way.** Partner-visible and consumer-only data are separated in the data model from the start, so the partner feed is clean the day it opens.

### The honest cost of C

- **No B2B revenue at launch.** Research's "margin engine" doesn't run until gates clear, so retail carries the business, against a ₹0 Pulse anchor.
- **stockinsights.ai keeps its head start** in the API lane.
- **The gates may take a long time.** Counsel and licensing are not on our clock.

### What would change this recommendation

- Counsel says crowd voting does **not** create an association problem, and rights are clear → move to A sooner.
- Counsel says it **does** → choose between E, D, or revisiting D-011. That will be its own decision.
- A broker or fintech offers to pay before launch → test the gates against a real deal.
